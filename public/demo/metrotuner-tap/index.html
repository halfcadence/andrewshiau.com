1 · What tapping means while the metronome runs

01Current — retime instantly, every tap each tap applies the running average immediately: the grid re-anchors per tap and the pendulum's parity math tears. Live-reproduced. The one virtue: zero latency between tap and tempo.
02At the next beat — the pick, now live taps collect; the new tempo takes effect exactly ON the next strike — the arm always finishes the sweep it is in, at the rate it started it, then turns at the new rate. No tear, ~one beat of latency. The DJ-mixer rule.
03At the next bar the musical version of 02: the current BAR plays out at the old tempo and the new one starts on the next downbeat — you tap during bar N, bar N+1 is in your tempo. Most peaceful; up to a bar of latency, which a musician expects and a drummer demands.
04The tap conducts while you tap, YOU are the metronome: the clicks yield (mute), the pendulum strikes on each of your taps — then when you stop for a beat, it relaunches at your tempo, downbeat where your hand left off. Zero fight by construction; the machine audibly defers to you. Cost: the silence can read as breakage the first time.
05Taps stop it your suggestion, plain: the first tap while running STOPS the metronome (the sweep finishes into its end), and your taps then set the tempo in silence; press start when ready. The simplest contract — tap never fights the clock because the clock yields entirely. Cost: an extra press to resume.

All five run the real engine shape — a lookahead scheduler on the audio clock, the pendulum phase-locked to played ticks — so what you hear and see is what would ship. The pick applies to the live instrument's tap; typing a bpm mid-run keeps today's immediate-but-anchored behavior (typing is one change, not eight in four seconds).
