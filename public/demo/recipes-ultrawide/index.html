Canelé099

ingredients

340gmilk
38gheavy cream
45gbutter
1vanilla bean (or 8g vanilla extract)
170ggranulated sugar
2gfleur de sel
1whole egg (~50g)
2egg yolks (~36g)
40gaged rum
85gAP flour
15gcornstarch
Nonstick spray (or beeswax) for molds

steps

  1. In a pot, combine milk, cream, butter, and vanilla. Heat to 175°F — do not boil. Remove from heat. Let infuse 5 minutes.

  2. In a bowl, whisk sugar, salt, eggs, and yolks together.

  3. Slowly pour about ¼ of the hot milk into the egg mixture, whisking constantly to temper. Sift in flour and cornstarch together, whisk until smooth.

  4. Gradually add the remaining milk, whisking until fully combined. Stir in rum.

  5. Strain through a sieve. Refrigerate overnight (24 hours minimum).

  6. Coat canelé molds with nonstick spray (beeswax also works but isn’t needed here). Stir rested batter gently. Strain again if lumpy.

  7. Preheat oven to 525°F. Fill each mold with 70g of batter.

  8. Bake at 525°F for 11 minutes. Without opening the oven, drop to 375°F and continue baking for 50–55 minutes.

  9. Unmold immediately onto a cooling rack upside down. Cool 2 hours. Eat the same day.

analysis

Total batter weight: 821g (excludes mold coating)

Structure solids (flour + cornstarch)100g12.2%
Fat (butter, cream, milk, yolk)79g9.6%
Sugar (added + milk lactose)188g22.9%
Liquid (milk, cream, rum, egg water)421g51.3%
Egg/protein25g3.0%

Structure at 12.2% lands inside the custardy sweet spot (8–14%) — softer and more pudding-like than the cakey canelé’s 16.8%. The cornstarch share sets the custard without gluten, so it stays tender.

Notes

  • A soft, custardy canelé — lower flour and a touch of cornstarch for a tender, pudding-like interior. Makes ~12. For a firmer version, see the Cakey Canelé.
  • Lower flour (85g) plus 15g cornstarch puts total starch at ~12% of batter (vs ~17% in the cakey version) for a soft, very custardy interior.
  • The cornstarch sets the custard without forming gluten, so it firms gently rather than toughening.
  • The high-heat start prevents mushrooming — this lower-solids batter needs a hotter shock (525°F) to set the shell fast.

Spirits in Baking

A practical guide to which spirits work best in which baked goods, based on testing and common sense. The goal: pick the right bottle so the flavor complements rather than fights the other ingredients.

What alcohol actually does in baking

Flavor — the residual aroma after most alcohol evaporates. After 60 min at 350°F+, only ~5% of the alcohol remains, but the volatile flavor compounds linger.

Crust formation — alcohol evaporates faster than water, drying the outer surface and helping create crisp shells.

Gluten inhibition — unlike water, alcohol doesn’t contribute to gluten formation, keeping batters tender.

The spirits

Dark rum

Flavor profile: Molasses, caramel, tropical fruit, brown sugar. Best with: vanilla, caramel, banana, coconut, plain custard.

The canelé classic. Rum’s molasses depth is what makes a traditional canelé taste like a canelé. Avoid with chocolate — it gets lost or clashes.

Brandy / Cognac

The chocolate match. Grape-based fruitiness complements cocoa like chocolate-covered cherries. It supports rather than competes.
