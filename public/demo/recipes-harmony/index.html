Two ultrawide layouts that aren’t finished

The spread ships, but the two halves don’t feel like a pair, and the colophon pages don’t use the width at all. Two questions, one sheet. Every option is rendered at a real 1881 px and scaled down, so the proportions are true.

Options are clickable — the sheet copies your picks itself. Toggle your OS light/dark to check both.

1  The recipe spread — how do the two halves become a pair?

Ingredients are short, steps are long, so the right half hangs far below the left and the two read as two unrelated columns rather than one spread.

Settled — round 1 The hairline across both heads (03/06) is in. 01–06 stay for reference; the live question is 07–10, which all keep the rule and vary only how the width divides.

Measured across all 124 recipes at 1881 px: median left 229px, right 461px — a 2.0× ratio. The right half is taller on 113 recipes, worst 5.2× (matcha-chiffon-cake: 166px vs 865px). But 6 recipes are the other way round — ishikari-nabe is 641px of ingredients against 394px of steps — so nothing here may assume steps are the long side.

01Current — equal halves Two 12-column fields, each the width the whole page used to be. The heads align but nothing else does — the left half stops and 230px of white runs down beside four more steps.

steps

  1. Steep 8g earl grey in 90g hot water for 5 minutes. Strain, cool.

  2. Whisk yolks with 60g sugar until pale and thick.

  3. Stream in oil, then the cooled infusion.

  4. Sift in flour; whisk to a smooth batter.

  5. Whip whites with cream of tartar to soft peaks.

  6. Add sugar in three additions; whip to firm peaks.

  1. Fold a third of the meringue into the base to loosen it.

  2. Fold in the rest in two additions, stopping while streaks remain.

  3. Pour into an ungreased tube pan from 20cm up.

  4. Bake 165°C for 45 minutes until the top springs back.

  5. Invert immediately onto its feet; cool completely upside down.

  6. Release with a thin knife. Serve plain.

Round 3 — the split decided by the recipe, not by me

Every option above sets one split for all 124 recipes, which is why each has a cost: the same geometry that fixes a 12-step cake leaves a 3-step cocktail worse off. These three switch on the step count instead. It is deterministic and needs no JavaScript — :has(> ol > li:nth-child(6)) means “six or more steps”, and I checked its count against the real DOM on 124 of 124 recipes.

Where the threshold comes from, measured at 1881 px on all 124 pages: splitting the steps at 4+ gives the least empty paper (103 px mean) but leaves 27 recipes with a two-step column, which doesn’t read as a column. 6+ is the first threshold where that never happens: 0 columns under three steps, 66 of 124 recipes split, empty paper down from 256 px to 126 and the worst case from 783 to 420. Ingredient count is not a second trigger — a single <table> won’t break across a column, so the only place the ingredient side can divide is a group boundary, and doing it there measured no better than today (167 vs 163 px on those 18 recipes) while cutting the step measure to 510 px.

116+ steps split — keep the 34rem measure Three 8-track bays (8 + 1 + 8 + 1 + 8 = 26). A bay is 548 px, which is exactly the 34rem measure the site already caps every step at — so the reading line is untouched and the second column is free. Recipes with five steps or fewer are bit-for-bit what ships today. Cost, and the render shows it: the number hangs at its column’s right edge, which in two columns is 20 px from the next column’s text — 01 reads as if it belongs to 05. This is the option that has the flaw, and 12 and 13 are the two ways out.

tortilla034

ingredients

steps

8
large eggs
Kosher salt
475g
extra-virgin olive oil
700g
Yukon Gold potatoes, thinly sliced
350g
yellow onions, thinly sliced
Aioli, for serving

  1. Beat eggs vigorously with a large pinch of salt until frothy. Set aside.

  2. Heat olive oil in a 10-inch nonstick skillet over medium-high until shimmering. Add potatoes and onions. Regulate heat so they bubble gently but not vigorously. Cook, stirring occasionally, until meltingly tender, about 25 minutes.

  3. Drain potatoes and onions through a fine-mesh strainer over a heatproof bowl. Reserve the oil. Season generously with salt.

  4. Re-froth the eggs, then fold in the potato-onion mixture. Stir until thoroughly combined. Rest 5 minutes.

  5. Wipe out skillet. Add 45g reserved oil and heat until shimmering. Scrape in the egg mixture. Cook, shaking and swirling the pan, until bottom and sides begin to set, about 3 minutes. Press edges inward to form a puck.

  6. Place a large flat plate over the skillet and, in one quick motion, invert the tortilla onto it. Add 15g more oil. Slide the tortilla back in. Cook until the second side is lightly browned but the centre is still tender, about 4 minutes.

  7. Slide or invert onto a serving plate. Rest at least 5 minutes. Serve with aioli. Equally good at room temperature.
