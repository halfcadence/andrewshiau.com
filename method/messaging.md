# Messaging side — the handoff is the last step of the work

Load with [method.md](method.md) whenever work leaves your hands: a message, a code
review, a doc, a write-up, a read-out.

The instrument on this side is **the sentence as a diagnostic**. If you cannot say it
plainly, you have not finished thinking it. Unclear writing is not a presentation problem
to fix later; it is the first symptom of an unfinished thought, and the cheapest one to
catch.

## How a message gets written

1. **Answer first.** Lead with the conclusion, then support it. A reader builds a
   hierarchy out of whatever order you hand them, so hand them the true one. The order
   you discovered something is almost never the order to explain it.
2. **Ask the four questions of every sentence.** What am I trying to say? What words
   express it? What would make it clearer? Is this fresh enough to land? A sentence that
   survives those needs no further polish.
3. **Say the strong thing, then say how sure you are.** Confidence and accuracy are
   separate dials. "The reinstall is redundant and unsafe. We have not pinned why the race
   stopped, so we are not claiming we fixed it" is both bold and honest. Hedging is a
   skill; hedging *instead of* claiming is a retreat.
4. **Name the ask.** Decide what you want back — an opinion, an owner, a decision, nothing
   — and cut what doesn't serve it.
5. **Cut, then cut the thing you're proud of.** The detail hardest to remove is usually the
   one that cost the most to find. Its cost to the reader is unrelated to its cost to you.

## Stated as constraints

- **A phrase that arrived pre-assembled did not come from your thinking.** Ready-made
  wording writes your sentence for you, and conceals your meaning from you as well as the
  reader. That is what makes it comfortable and what makes it worth deleting.
- **Prefer the word you can picture.** Abstraction is where meaning goes to hide.
- **Short word over long, active over passive, everyday over jargon** — as tells, not laws.
  A passive is a flag to check, not a fault to fix.
- **The artifact carries the detail; the message carries the ask.** If it's linked, don't
  summarize it.
- **Keep the judgments, cut the derivations.** "Best value for the effort" is a conclusion
  the reader can't reach alone. How you got there belongs in the doc.
- **Vagueness is where responsibility hides.** When a sentence would read as blame, the fix
  is to aim it at the missing mechanism, not to fog it: "a flagged risk had nowhere to go,"
  not "it was flagged and shipped anyway."
- **A condition is not a cause.** "It's complex" and "be more careful" name no mechanism
  and fix nothing.

## Anti-patterns

- Explaining the mechanism in the message that links the doc explaining it.
- Structure that follows your model of the work rather than the reader's decision.
- A summary that is a second, worse copy of the artifact.
- Ending on your own open worry — it reads as fishing for reassurance and buries the ask.
- Asking for "any feedback." Nobody knows what that means. Ask for the specific thing.
- Polishing an internal note past the point of usefulness. A typo costs nothing; a day of
  delay costs a day.
