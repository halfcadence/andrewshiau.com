# Designer side — taste finds the simplest form

Load with [method.md](method.md) for design, visual, layout, or copy work.

The instrument on this side is **the eye**. A design decision is made by putting the
candidates next to each other, rendered for real, and looking. Not by describing them.

## How a design decision gets made

1. **Render every option, in the real system.** The actual typeface, tokens, grid, and
   breakpoints it will ship in. A proof sheet, in the print sense: a trial pull you
   check before committing.
2. **Option 1 is always what's live today.** The rest are read against it. Without the
   baseline, "better" has no referent.
3. **A few distinct options beat a dozen near-duplicates.** If two options differ by
   2px, they are one option.
4. **Pick by looking.** Then state the one-line reason, after the choice, not before.
5. **Check both colorways and phone width** before calling it done. Most layout bugs
   live in the second colorway or under 480px.

## Taste, stated as constraints

Restraint is the default, not a style choice.

- **Structure is rules, not boxes.** A wrap-around hairline border to fake a card is
  the tell. Use a top rule, a gutter, whitespace.
- **Exactly one accent hue,** in a handful of sanctioned roles, and nowhere else. The
  accent is the same hue in both colorways; only the base tones invert.
- **Hierarchy comes from size, weight, and position** — not from color, not from boxes.
- **The empty modules are the composition.** Whitespace is placed, not left over.
- **Ration the delights.** A few deliberate ones read as care; scattered ones read as
  noise.
- **One filled form per page,** for the key statement. Everything else is type + rule.
- **Detail is where credibility lives.** Optical alignment, baseline, one type ramp.

## Anti-patterns

- Decorating instead of deciding — adding a treatment because the space felt empty.
- Describing options in prose and asking someone to imagine them.
- A new component when a rule and a gutter would do.
- A second accent color "just for this one case."
- Judging a design in a fixture that isn't the real system.

## Copy is design

Plain, dry, understated. No preamble, no "in today's world", nothing that sounds like a
LinkedIn post. Own the premise plainly rather than reaching for a softer word. State
what was done; let the reader draw the conclusion.
